## Business requirement description
QuickShip Logistics is a company that provides freight delivery services to individual customers, online shops, and businesses. The company picks up parcels at its warehouses or at the sender's location, transports them through a network of transit warehouses, and delivers them to recipients nationwide.

Currently, order intake, shipping fee calculation, driver assignment, shipment tracking, and complaint handling are all done manually. Staff record order information in notebooks, look up shipping rates by region and weight from printed tables, call drivers by phone to assign pickups, and update delivery statuses via text messages. When customers file complaints about damaged or lost goods, staff must search through paper records to process compensation.

As order volume has grown rapidly, the manual process has caused many problems: incorrect fee calculations, overlapping driver assignments, inability to determine which warehouse a parcel is currently at, slow complaint resolution, and difficulty reconciling cash-on-delivery (COD) collections. The company wants to build a database system to manage the entire shipping process from the moment a parcel is received until it is successfully delivered.

The Operations Director provides the following requirement summary:
The company wants to develop a freight management system covering order management, shipping rate tables, transit warehouses, drivers, shipment tracking, COD collection, and complaint handling with compensation.
The system stores employee information including employee ID, full name, email, phone number, position, assigned warehouse, and status. An employee may be a reception clerk, warehouse staff, driver, customer service representative, accountant, or warehouse manager.
The system manages customer (sender) information including customer ID, full name or company name, email, phone number, default address, customer type (individual, online shop, business), and membership tier (standard, silver, gold, diamond). The membership tier is determined based on the total number of orders and total accumulated shipping fees within the most recent 12 months. Customers with a higher tier receive a greater discount rate on shipping fees.
The company operates a nationwide network of transit warehouses. Each warehouse has a warehouse code, warehouse name, address, province/city, region (North, Central, South), warehouse type (origin warehouse, transit warehouse, destination warehouse), maximum capacity (in parcels), current parcel count, manager, and status (active, temporarily closed, under maintenance). Parcels move through multiple transit warehouses before reaching the destination warehouse nearest to the recipient.
When a customer sends a parcel, staff create an order with the following information: order ID (tracking number), sender customer, recipient name, recipient phone number, recipient address, recipient province/city, goods type (documents, electronics, fragile items, food, bulky items, general goods), weight, dimensions (length × width × height), number of packages, declared goods value, pickup method (drop-off at warehouse or door-to-door pickup), delivery method (door-to-door delivery or warehouse pickup), whether COD collection is required, COD amount, special notes, and receiving warehouse. Order statuses include: awaiting pickup, picked up, in transit, arrived at destination warehouse, out for delivery, delivered successfully, delivery failed, returning to sender, returned to sender, and cancelled.
Shipping fees are calculated based on a rate table. The rate table is determined by the origin region, destination region, goods type, and weight bracket (e.g., 0–0.5 kg, 0.5–1 kg, 1–3 kg, 3–5 kg, 5–10 kg, over 10 kg). If the volumetric weight (length × width × height ÷ 5000) is greater than the actual weight, the system uses the volumetric weight for fee calculation. In addition to the base shipping fee, surcharges may apply for fragile items, bulky items, door-to-door pickup, after-hours delivery, or cargo insurance. Customers with higher membership tiers receive a discount on the total shipping fee.
When an order requires door-to-door pickup, the system needs to assign a driver to pick up the parcel. Each driver has a driver ID, assigned warehouse, service area, vehicle type (motorcycle, small truck, large truck), license plate number, status (available, picking up, delivering, on leave), and maximum orders per day. The system may only assign a driver whose service area matches the pickup/delivery address. A driver who is on leave or has reached the maximum number of orders for the day cannot accept additional orders.
The system tracks the shipment journey through the transit warehouse network. Each time a parcel moves (from one warehouse to another, or from a warehouse to a driver for delivery), the system records a tracking entry consisting of: the order, origin warehouse, destination warehouse, transporting driver, departure time, arrival time, leg status (in transit, arrived, delayed, incident), and notes. This allows customers to look up where their parcel is at any given time.
When a driver delivers a parcel, the system records the delivery result: successful delivery (recording the person who signed for receipt and the delivery time) or failed delivery (reason: unreachable by phone, recipient absent, wrong address, recipient refused). If delivery fails, the system allows rescheduling delivery up to a maximum of 3 attempts. After 3 failed delivery attempts, the order transitions to returning-to-sender status.
For COD orders, when delivery is successful the driver collects payment from the recipient. The system records the COD amount collected for each order. At the end of each day, the accountant reconciles the total COD amount collected by each driver against the amount the driver submits back. After reconciliation, the company transfers the COD funds to the sender customer on a cycle basis (daily or weekly depending on the membership tier). Each COD transfer generates a disbursement record containing: recipient customer, list of related orders, total COD amount, COD service fee, actual amount transferred, transfer method, transfer date, and status.
The system supports complaint management. Customers can file complaints for orders with the following types: damaged goods, lost goods, wrong delivery, late delivery, driver conduct, or incorrect shipping fee. Each complaint records: complaint ID, related order, complaining customer, complaint type, detailed description, attached images (if any), creation date, assigned handler, status (new, in progress, awaiting evidence, resolved, compensated, rejected), resolution result, and compensation amount (if applicable). The compensation amount must not exceed the declared goods value on the order.
The system must maintain historical records of orders, shipment tracking, deliveries, COD payments, and complaints. Staff should be able to view daily order reports, successful delivery rate, failed delivery rate, shipping fee revenue, COD reconciliation status, and complaint statistics by type.
The main goal of the system is to help the company manage freight operations efficiently, calculate shipping fees accurately, track shipment journeys in real time, reconcile COD collections transparently, and handle complaints promptly.
